Plato's Cave

I use photography and image making to express my perspective on the world. Moments and scenes and how they are captured on camera, their perspective, is the unique artisanship of photography. Likewise, image making, the intentional collaboration of visuals, allows us as artists to express our perspectives. The directive nature of the medium allows me to express myself with the visuals that surround me. Though we are in an age where everyone has a camera in their pocket, as artists, direction and perspective is the artistic distinction. When images are captured, intention is what is important. Intention is what sets me apart from Aunt Sally taking a group photo at the family function, though it is arguable that she would be an artist too. I digress as “what makes an image art?” or “what is art?” is another (complicated) ball of wax. The question at hand is how do I visually represent the world. I do this by translating my influences into my work. Everything we are exposed to influences us because the world is what we make sense of as artists, so everything my senses absorb influences the way I make images through any medium.

Photography, image making, or the combination of image and text sponsor my ability as an artist to express myself and my perspective on the notions of our world today. In a world that is so complex and out of our individual control, image making of any sort is the avenue for making sense of the world’s complexities. Personally I like to work with mixed media projects especially physically (not digital). I like working with physical prints and materials to play with visuals in hand. I like the personal and unique expression of this practice and the result of the work. I find working by hand to be the best way for me to express my perspectives of the world, my vision. It feels human to me and that perspective is what is reflected in my creations. Even the artistic preference to work with paper and the reasons for which is also an artistic choice that reflects perspective and influence. So, it is impossible to truly put into words how I or anyone exactly translates their worldview into the images that they make. However, for me I can say that exposure to other artists’ work in any medium (not just imaging) can inspire new perspectives within me as an artist.

As we are in the digital age, the world is evermore difficult to navigate. The artistic benefit of this is that now more than ever there is opportunity to create and more complex mediums to create with. While I do find it daunting to explore creating digitally sometimes (most of my technical photography experience is manual), I do believe it is important to remain with the times and progress with technology, even as an artist, not that there is not a strong statement in not doing as I mentioned before. Thus, regardless of the medium, I think art is the best way to express our world views.

AI Slop

Nesrine Malik’s article, “With ‘AI slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster”, published in The Guardian, truly outlines the powerful capabilities of AI. Not unlike just the orthodox use of social media to create art and media on a public forum, Ai has significantly changed the game. The common fear of the power of social media, formally “anyone can post anything whether it's true or not”, has morphed. Now anyone can manufacture media fast and easily. This has manifested in many dark ways, especially as the article outlined through mass distribution of hyperbolic and/or outlandish propaganda. In my opinion, it is a dangerous tool being mishandled and abused. AI, as controversial as it may be, has the capacity to compute and manufacture data, information, and media at a rate that we cannot as humans. However, as manipulative or manipulated the tool may be, it is important not to overlook that the creations of AI are a product of an individual’s input to the system. Though AI is the creator, there is a human person who inspired the outlandish AI videos we see on the internet. That is not something to disregard, and with the nature of social media (it being a global public forum) it is impossible who starts the rotation of AI videos. Additionally, AI images and videos are evolving. The more it creates the more it learns how to create more realistic and believable images and videos. This is what is scary. It is becoming harder and harder to create digitally and/or share online as AI dominates these spaces now. As the article mentioned, it is even embedded in our politics. Heavily. I do believe there is hope for a creative and artistic renaissance. There is a lot of AI rejection especially among young adults and among many artists. There is a possibility that as AI further infiltrates the online spaces for artists and creators people will lean further into real life mediums. Traditional “paper” methods, live performance, in person showings and installations, etc will boom. The artistic world may be straying away from online spaces. Alternatively, as some artists have already begun doing, creators will further embrace AI and use it as a tool. However, I would not consider embracing AI creations as art a renaissance. If that were to be the case it would totally alter the meaning of what art is. It would be the dawn of a new artistic movement where art and media isn’t necessarily human. In that universe, what we think of now as “art” or a creative work will manifest in new mediums or simply a new term. Likely what will actually be the case is that all of this will be true in the future. What is undoubtable is that AI will only continue to grow more powerful and influential.

The Guardian

This was such an interesting journalistic project. What a compelling medium for journalism. Using the graphic novel style was a brilliant way to convey a graphic and sensitive story. It truly gave a clear visual story of what happened and why it is so important. It was also nice that towards the end there were some real pictures of the hospital and the woman and her baby. Putting those at the end really brought the story to life. The overall design was very cohesive and simple. The drawings for each scene were very bare-bones and simple, allowing the reader to take in the story while leaving a little room for one’s imagination to fill in the details. The design of the site was also very easy to navigate. I liked how it was kind of set up like an Instagram story compilation. Each slide had a little timer at the top so you didn’t even need to “turn the page” it would go for you almost like watching a graphic novel storyfilm. I thought that was cool. It is also convenient as the story model would be easily transferable to social media. As it is a work of journalism this is definitely important in today’s world. Overall, I really enjoyed the story and the way it was told. I am now curious about multi-media storytelling, especially in journalism. What an enticing way to engage an otherwise doomscrolling audience.

New York Times

The design from the beginning part of the article was very compelling. The deep, dimensional red background along with the scrollable texts really grabbed my attention and set the tone. This sort of design brings a whole new meaning to the traditional newspaper headline and blurb combo. It is clean and compelling, a great way to pull a reader in. The second section of graphics was so unexpected and shocking. Every scroll kept building. Beginning with the black screen and missile graphics was a very nice touch, when the explosion started it was so unexpected. It was so immersive and intricate. It was so cool that as you scroll through it feels like you’re moving through the explosion. It was enveloping. The last section with the moving map was also very cool. As you scrolled the data expanded. Red was a great choice for the dots and details on the graphic as it has an obvious connotation and it was seen in the beginning graphics. Even though there was a difference in the function of each of the graphic sections, there was a cohesive theme among them. Red was a good, strong choice. Overall, this was a very powerful way to represent such a serious issue. The illustration, especially in the second section, was very well done. Because of the point of view component to this section, it really helped bring the example to life. It was also cool that you could see the individual frames click as you scroll through the illustration in the second section. It is like a digital flipbook in a sense. I am definitely curious about how to animate that and create a piece that moves like that. It was like being inside a comic book while reading an article, so so immersive. From the writing the point of the article seemed to be to enlighten the reader to the realities of nuclear weaponry and the impact it would have if these missiles were ever launched. Absolutely it did just that. It was also a strong choice to have three graphic sections. This marked a clear beginning, middle, and end which helped shape the narrative of the article. This is important because the graphics are there to support the written story, not to distract or take away from the motive of the writing. This is not a form I could have imagined for a New York Times article, but it is genius. In the age of social media and chronic electronic use, it is important to find ways, especially in journalism, to digitize and adapt the new expectations of digital media. Using digital art as a way to enhance the experience of reading a digital article is a brilliant idea. I can imagine this kind of feature pulls in readers who may not have otherwise decided to read an article like this. Maybe this is the new “hook” method?
